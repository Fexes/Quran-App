# Prayer and Wudu guide research

Checked 21 October 2026. The app content is a concise Sunni introduction, with a complete two-rak‘ah example for someone praying alone and an ordinary water-based wudu sequence. It does not label every individual detail a universally agreed pillar. Instructions and English meanings are newly written; Arabic Quran and hadith recitations retain their transmitted wording. Full recitations stay available offline.

## Prayer

Five chapters: preparation; standing and recitation; bowing and standing upright; two prostrations with a sitting between; final sitting and salam. The last chapter explains the third and fourth rak‘ahs. The ordinary resident counts are explicitly fard only. Witr is separate and not described as universally optional.

### Differences kept visible

- [Dar Al-Ifta on resting or folding hands](https://dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/19034/حكم-إرسال-اليدين-في-الصلاة) supports acknowledging Maliki practice and avoiding one exclusive hand position.
- [Dar Al-Ifta on Amin](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/18478/حكم-التأمين-بعد-قراءة-سورة-الفاتحة-في-الصلاة) distinguishes schools and prayer contexts.
- [Dar Al-Ifta, fatwa 5833](https://dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16470/حكم-تحريك-الأصبع-في-التشهد-وكيفيته) explains recognized differences in the index finger’s timing and movement.
- [Faraz Rabbani’s Hanafi answer on women’s prayer](https://seekersguidance.org/uncategorized/what-are-the-differences-between-the-prayer-of-a-man-and-a-woman-in-the-hanafi-school/) treats posture differences as optimality, not a different prayer sequence. Images are illustrative, not exclusive validity conditions.
- Three repetitions of bowing/prostration dhikr are recommended here. The guide does not claim exactly three is a universal requirement. It also does not reduce all spoken remembrances to optional acts.

## Wudu

Three chapters cover intention, hands, mouth and nose; face, complete arms including hands and elbows, whole head and ears; feet including ankles, heels and toes, then recommended remembrance. One complete wash is distinguished from three recommended washes. The head is wiped once in this chosen method, not washed three times.

### Editorial boundaries

The app does not assert that all touch, all sleep, all bleeding, or touching private parts universally breaks wudu. It directs users to their school for those conditions. Ghusl, tayammum, footwear/dressing concessions, continuing discharge, and exceptional prayer cases need separate instruction. The guide avoids invented supplications for each washed limb. Learned ritual Arabic is retained, while English meanings are concise original renderings rather than copied translations.

## Verification

Content and source identifiers are checked together before handoff. This work adds no tests and runs no simulators. Arabic texts were compared with the cited primary text; no claim of formal scholarly certification is made.
